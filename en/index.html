Hidden control in the legal economy

I study how criminal and high-risk actors enter, use and distort lawful economic systems. My work focuses on companies, ownership structures, market opacity, sanctions and anti-money laundering, combining criminological questions with empirical and computational methods.

02 / Current research

Current research directions

The portfolio is organised around a continuing research programme rather than only completed outputs.

Conceptual & comparative

Criminal infiltration beyond mafia labels

Comparing forms of criminal participation in the legal economy across different actors, conducts and market contexts, with a systematic review designed to make the evidence base reproducible.

Empirical

Ownership, hidden control and market effects

Studying how high-risk ownership structures, indirect control and opaque connections relate to firm, asset and market outcomes.

Policy & prevention

AML capacity in public institutions

Examining how public-sector actors can identify money-laundering exposure, operationalise preventive duties and turn fragmented information into measurable risk analysis.

04 / Research approach

Questions first, methods second

Methods are selected to answer substantive questions about hidden influence in lawful systems, not treated as an end in themselves.

Research questions

  • Criminal infiltration in lawful marketsHow illicit actors enter, consolidate and distort formally legal economic activity.
  • Ownership and hidden controlHow direct and indirect structures conceal influence, dependency or effective control.
  • Sanctions and AML riskHow corporate and financial arrangements facilitate, conceal or mitigate high-risk activity.

Analytical approach

  • Structured and linked dataIntegration of company, ownership, case and contextual information.
  • Network and ownership reconstructionTracing relationships across entities and estimating direct and indirect influence.
  • Case-based indicator designUsing empirical cases to derive transparent and testable risk indicators.
05 / About

Research profile

My research sits at the intersection of criminology, economic analysis and financial-crime prevention. I am particularly interested in the point at which formally lawful companies, assets and transactions become vehicles for criminal influence, hidden control or regulatory circumvention.

I combine quantitative and case-based evidence to connect criminological theory with questions that can be examined at company, market and network level. A recurring aim is to make complex economic relationships easier to inspect, compare and translate into practical risk analysis.
