Research profile

Research at the intersection of criminology, economic analysis and financial-crime prevention, with particular focus on the use of formally lawful companies, assets and transactions as vehicles for criminal influence, hidden control or regulatory circumvention. The methodological approach combines structured company and ownership data, network reconstruction, proxy-based screening and case-based indicator design.

Current research directions

Conceptual
Criminal infiltration beyond mafia labels

Comparative and systematic synthesis of criminal participation in the legal economy across actors, conducts and market contexts.

Empirical
Ownership, hidden control and market effects

Analysis of high-risk ownership structures, indirect control and opaque connections at firm, asset and market level.

Policy
AML capacity in public institutions

Operationalisation of preventive duties, identification of exposure and use of fragmented public information for measurable risk analysis.
